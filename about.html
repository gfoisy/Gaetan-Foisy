A little bit about me

I'm Gaetan Foisy, a mechanical engineer in Austin, Texas. I design lighting and shading products for high-volume manufacturing at Lutron Electronics, and in my spare time I work in extremely low volumes: I build the machines and tools I need for my own projects. What I enjoy most is closing the gap between a design on the screen and a part that works in the real world.


At work

I have worked at Lutron since 2021. The project I'm proudest of is the adjustment mechanism for the D2R, the remodel version of Lutron's D2 luminaire. I had a 1/8" perimeter around the light engine for a mechanism that controls the luminaire's rotation, its tilt, and its distance from the ceiling. Tilt and height had to be coupled so that the optic and light beam behave exactly like the D2 product line, because a D2R can be installed right next to a full D2. On top of that, the luminaire had to install into and remove from the ceiling, lock in place at any adjustment, and dissipate 20 watts of heat to keep the LED below its temperature limit while surrounded by insulation in an enclosed ceiling. And it had to be cheap to manufacture. My team and I delivered all of it.



Outside of work

I keep building at home, and many of my projects end up making tools for other projects. I used a 3D printer to build a better 3D printer, built a CNC router that machined its own parts, and turned a broken 3D printer and a USB microscope into a $300 vision measuring machine.


I also co-run Over_Engineered. My business partner handles most of the business side and aesthetic design, and I handle mechanical design and prototyping. We have shipped several desk organizers and an incense holder, with more in the pipeline. We also take on consulting work. For Yeoyu, I redesigned their coffee dripper for manufacturability and extraction. The original frame was machined from a solid block of metal, which was expensive and wasteful. I turned it into a deep-drawn sheet metal part, added a modular "puck" to control the coffee flow (and with it, the brew's total dissolved solids), and designed a custom silicone gasket to seal the puck to the frame. I also vetted the vendors, led the design for manufacturability, and handled quality control.



How I work

My method for hard problems is to keep slamming my head into them until they stop being problems. Just as important, I come up for air regularly to re-evaluate and make sure I'm slamming my head into the right places.



Where I come from

I grew up an engineer. I used a ball-peen hammer as a teddy bear and never looked back. I learned to weld in my grandfather's shop, mostly while helping him repair his continuously breaking tractors. I didn't realize engineering was a career until I took an engineering class in high school, and after that I spent most of my study halls in that classroom and the shop next door. In college, I led the frame team for our Baja SAE car, designing and welding the frame, and I spent nine months as a co-op at Gleason.



What I'm looking for

I want to keep sharpening my skills on new problems, and to spend more of my time building things with my hands, closing the loop between a design on the computer and a part in the real world.



When I'm not engineering

I make pottery, travel, scuba dive (I'm certified for deep diving), and run long distances.
